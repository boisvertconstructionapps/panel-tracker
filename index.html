Phases and voltages

What each system is

  • Single-phase 120/240 — houses and small shops. Two hot wires. 120V from either hot to neutral, 240V between them.
  • Single-phase 120/208 (network) — apartments and some commercial suites fed from a larger 3-phase service. Still only two hots in this panel. 120V to neutral, 208V between the hots.
  • 3-phase 208Y/120 — stores and light commercial. Three hots. 120V to neutral, 208V between any two, 208V 3-phase for equipment.
  • 3-phase 480Y/277 — larger buildings. 277V to neutral (often lighting), 480V between hots, 480V 3-phase.
  • 3-phase 240V high-leg delta — older commercial. A and C are 120V to neutral. The high leg is about 208V to neutral and to ground. Between hots is 240V. Three-pole equipment is 240V 3-phase.

How the rows are phased

Odd slots are the left column, even slots the right. One row is a left slot and the slot beside it, and both sides of that row are the same phase. The phase comes from the real slot number, so turning the panel upside down does not change which slot is which phase.

  • Single-phase: A, B, A, B… down the panel.
  • 3-phase: A, B, C, A, B, C… down the panel.

Which breakers go where

  • 1-pole uses one row. Voltage to neutral: 120V on 120/240, 120/208, 208Y, and on A or C of a high-leg panel; 277V on 480Y; 208V to neutral on the high leg.
  • 2-pole uses two rows down the same column. 240V on 120/240 and high-leg, 208V on 120/208 and 208Y, 480V on 480Y.
  • 3 spaces in a 3-phase panel is a 3-pole, 3-phase breaker on its own. 208V, 480V, or 240V 3-phase, matching the system. No extra prompt.
  • A 3-pole breaker does not belong in a single-phase panel.

High-leg rules

  • The high leg is usually B, the middle phase. If the panel label says A or C, set High leg to match.
  • Mark the high leg orange.
  • Do not put 120V loads on the high leg.
  • A multi-pole breaker that lands on the high leg should be straight 240V-rated, not a 120/240 breaker.
  • The panel needs a caution that this phase is 208 volts to ground.

Tandems

Most 3-phase panels do not accept tandem (twin) breakers. Check the label on the panel.

Wire colors you will usually see

Colors are not the same on every job. Where more than one system is in the building, post the colors at the gear.

  • 120/240: black, red, white (neutral)
  • 208Y/120: black, red, blue, white
  • High-leg delta: black, orange (high leg), blue, white
  • 480Y/277: brown, orange, yellow, gray (neutral)

Reference only — follow NEC, panel label, and local code.
